Bitcoin event explorer

Explore thresholds within events and compare similar observations across dates. Prices are saved YES-token history, not executable quotes. Source: Binance BTC/USDT.

Event timeline and threshold curves

Heatmap: one row per threshold, color = YES price (pale → dark teal). Gray cells are missing or filtered observations. A small green tick marks the first observed hit. A check beside a threshold means venue recorded YES (rule version unverified). Click a cell to move the shared time marker. Curves use the latest completed bucket with age less than its actual width; missing thresholds stay disconnected.

Same threshold across event dates

Uses the current date, timeframe, direction and market filters. Date filters select windows overlapping the range and retain their full saved histories. Different event windows are separate conditions; overlapping dates alone do not establish implication. On the relative axis, times are aligned to each market's own deadline. The dashed marker follows the shared slider: the same UTC time, or the same remaining hours, across these markets.

Distance and time remaining across events

Color = mean YES price (pale → dark teal) in each cell. Hover for bucket count, contributing markets and events. Distance bins are 0.25 percentage points; time bins span one twenty-fourth of the selected horizon. Zero distance is the barrier. Unknown paths and missing BTC candles are excluded; after-hit observations follow the toggle above. Market buckets are correlated and are weighted equally.

How neighboring markets change together

Over the full saved window of the selected event and direction, compare up to eight thresholds nearest BTC at the slider time (or the median threshold if BTC is missing). Pearson correlation uses exact matched five-minute price changes, without bridging gaps. Positive lag pairs row-market A's change with column-market B's later change. Gray cells have fewer than 12 matched intervals or constant changes. These are descriptive associations, not evidence that a market predicts another.

Coverage and interpretation

The 0.05 filter uses full-history maxima and is retrospective. Restore the tails when inspecting the shape of an entire event. All historical rule versions remain unverified; green “venue recorded YES” badges describe the venue record. Observed hit times use the end of the first crossing Binance candle, not the venue resolution time. Creation-based windows have unresolved minute boundaries: their hit status remains unknown and they are excluded from path-dependent pooled analysis. No missing values are replaced with zero.
